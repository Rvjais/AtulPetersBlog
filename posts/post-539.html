Expert Advice on Gall Bladder Surgery: Insights from a Leading Gall Bladder Surgeon

Cholelithiasis or the presence of stones in the gall bladder is a common problem all over the world. In a few cases, it might also be associated with stones in the biliary tract.

Symptoms of Gall Bladder Stones

Usually, stones in the gall bladder are asymptomatic but few may present with pain and fever with or without jaundice.

Some patients present with complications associated with stones in the gall bladder the most serious being pancreatitis.

Pain is localized to the right upper abdomen and might radiate to the back and right shoulder. It usually begins after intake of fatty food typically lasts 1-5 hours, increases steadily over 10-20 minutes, and then gradually wanes.

Pain is constant and not relieved by vomiting or, antacids, or positional changes.

Also, there are some nonspecific symptoms (eg, indigestion, dyspepsia, belching, or bloating)?

Patients with asymptomatic gallstones have no abnormal findings on physical examination.

Patients with uncomplicated cholelithiasis or simple gallstones have normal laboratory test results

Diagnosis and Treatment

Ultrasonography is the procedure of choice to diagnose gallbladder stones.

The treatment of gallstones depends upon the stage of the disease. Symptomatic gallstones –need surgical intervention, known as Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy.

Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy is a daycare procedure in most cases. The patient gets admitted in the hospital on the day of surgery. The surgery is done through 3 to 4 small holes. The usual time is between 15 minutes to half an hour. There are usually no diet restrictions after surgery. The pain is minimal and the majority can be discharged on the same day of surgery.

Usually, there is only one follow-up visit and no antibiotics postoperatively.
